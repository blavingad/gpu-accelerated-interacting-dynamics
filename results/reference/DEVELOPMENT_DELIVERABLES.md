# What the digital-development methodology delivers

A completed design campaign should produce a reviewable package, not just a good-looking print:

1. **Requirements and domain:** geometry/field version, ink and nozzle envelope, font, target pitch and deposited-dot size, speed/DPI/frequency, clearance and controller constraints.
2. **Declared context pool:** explicit ordered preceding/current occupancy pairs, baseline cases, history assumptions, and the link from each printable glyph to its contexts.
3. **Design record:** isolated/full-stroke references, screened structures, accepted and rejected coupled optimization steps, and cost counters. Label limited screens and best-found candidates honestly.
4. **Controller realization:** shared context-dependent charge targets followed by waveform-aware voltage/integer-command realization, exact address ordering, bounds and replay. A net-charge optimum is not automatically a deployable command table.
5. **Assessment:** complete required-pool coverage, per-context/per-drop errors, arrival-time horizontal effects, throughput, target-eligible contact diagnostics, gutter deflection, numerical checks and explicit failures.
6. **Printer evidence:** frozen commands, operating settings, independent acquisition/metrology provenance, repeated-print uncertainty where available, and measured-versus-simulated errors separated from geometric-target errors.
7. **Reuse and economics:** versioned artifacts, recalibration conditions, engineering/rig/material/computation cost and a prospective comparison with the established development process.

For printhead development, geometry becomes an outer design variable. Each changed geometry needs an appropriate field solution and renewed checks of the force/charging closures. Cross-geometry capability is the intended extension, not an experimentally demonstrated result of the current two-configuration assessment.
